02 // STUTTGART LEGACY & ENGINEERING MINDS

FROM HEAVY ARMOR TO TRACK PERFECTION

1931 • STUTTGART

Dr. Ing. h.c. F. Porsche GmbH

Founded by Ferdinand Porsche as an independent motor vehicle development and consulting bureau, pioneering torsion-bar suspension, aerodynamic streamliners, and high-efficiency engineering principles that still define every 911.

1939–1945 • HEAVY ARMOR & TYPE 64

Wartime Engineering & Petrol-Electric Drive

Alongside the streamlined Type 64 racer, Porsche's engineering bureau designed heavy armored vehicles—including the Tiger (P) prototype, Ferdinand/Elefant, and Panzer VIII Maus—pioneering petrol-electric hybrid drive systems before returning exclusively to sports cars in 1948.

1948–1963 • THE 356 & 911

Birth of an Unmistakable Silhouette

Ferry Porsche realized his dream with the 1948 Porsche 356 "No. 1" Roadster, culminating in the 1963 debut of the air-cooled 911—establishing the rear-engine motorsport DNA carried forward to the 992 GT3 RS.

THE ARCHITECTS BEHIND THE 911 GT3 RS

DIRECTOR, GT MODEL LINE

Andreas Preuninger

Known as "Mr. GT," Preuninger led the Weissach GT division to abandon front luggage space in favor of a single, massive central angled radiator—unlocking active front-wing aerodynamics never before seen on a road-legal 911.

VP STYLE PORSCHE

Michael Mauer

Oversaw the uncompromising "form follows function" exterior language, integrating 911 RSR endurance racing aero elements seamlessly into the iconic 911 widebody silhouette.

BRAND AMBASSADOR & TEST DRIVER

Jörg Bergmeister

Piloted the 911 GT3 RS around the 20.8-km Nürburgring Nordschleife in a blistering 6:49.328 (6:44.848 on the 20.6-km layout), validating every damper setting and DRS transition on the limit.

POWERPLANT

4.0-Litre Naturally Aspirated Boxer-6

386 kW (525 PS) at 8,500 rpm with 465 Nm torque at 6,300 rpm. Equipped with motorsport-derived camshafts, individual throttle bodies, and rigid valve train.

ACCELERATION & BRAKING

0–100 km/h in 3.2 s • Top Speed 296 km/h

0–160 km/h in 6.9 s; 0–200 km/h in 10.6 s. Massive 408 mm front composite/steel rotors (optional 410 mm PCCB) deliver sub-30-meter 100–0 km/h stopping power.

TRANSMISSION

7-Speed Short-Ratio PDK

Shorter overall gear ratios than the standard 911 GT3 for explosive corner-exit punch, paired with a lightweight stainless-steel twin-tailpipe sports exhaust.

ACTIVE AERODYNAMICS

Formula 1-Inspired DRS System

Continuously adjustable front wing elements and a two-piece swan-neck rear wing generate 409 kg of downforce at 200 km/h and 860 kg at 285 km/h—triple the standard GT3.

COOLING ARCHITECTURE

Single Central Angled Radiator

Replaces the three-radiator layout and front trunk with a single large radiator borrowed from the Le Mans-winning 911 RSR, freeing space for active front aero flaps.

VORTEX MANAGEMENT

CFRP Roof Fins & Wheel Arch Louvers

Carbon-fiber roof fins direct warm radiator exhaust air outward so the rear wing ingests pure, cool ambient airflow, while fender louvers relieve wheel-well pressure.

SUSPENSION & STEERING

Double-Wishbone Front Axle & PASM

Aerodynamically shaped teardrop wishbone links add 40 kg of front downforce at top speed. Drivers can adjust rebound/compression damping and PTV+ differential directly from the steering wheel.

COCKPIT & INTERIOR

CFRP Full Bucket Seats & Race-Tex

Finished in black leather, microfibre Race-Tex, and satin carbon weave. Features a 360 mm GT3 steering wheel with four rotary mode dials and dedicated DRS button.

LIGHTWEIGHT CONSTRUCTION

Extensive Carbon-Fiber Reinforced Plastic

Doors, front wings, roof, front lid, and bucket seats are crafted from CFRP, keeping kerb weight to 1,450 kg (1,435 kg with Weissach magnesium wheels).
